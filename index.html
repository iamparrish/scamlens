LEARN TO SPOT THE SIGNS

Safety academy.

Short, practical lessons for recognizing common online fraud tactics.

LESSON 01 · PHISHING

Fake bank and KYC alerts

Unexpected messages may impersonate a bank and pressure you to update KYC or reveal credentials.

Safer move: Open your bank's official app or type its known website address yourself.
LESSON 02 · JOB FRAUD

Too-good-to-be-true jobs

Fake recruiters may promise high pay for simple tasks, then ask for registration or training fees.

Safer move: Verify the company through its official careers page. Be cautious about upfront fees.
LESSON 03 · UPI FRAUD

Payment requests and QR codes

A UPI PIN authorizes a payment. You do not need to enter it just to receive money.

Safer move: Check the recipient and amount. Never share your UPI PIN or OTP.
LESSON 04 · PRIZE SCAMS

Unexpected winnings

Lottery or prize messages you never entered can be used to collect fees or personal details.

Safer move: Do not pay to claim an unexpected prize. Verify with the official organizer.
LESSON 05 · OTP THEFT

One-time password requests

Fraudsters may impersonate support staff and ask for a code to access your account.

Safer move: Keep OTPs, passwords, PINs and recovery codes private.
LESSON 06 · URGENCY

Pressure and threats

Scam messages may create panic with account-closure threats, legal action or countdowns.

Safer move: Pause, verify independently and don't let urgency make the decision.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

Awareness is your first defense.

ScamLens is a frontend-only educational prototype demonstrating explainable checks for suspicious messages and URLs.

What ScamLens does

⌕
Pattern-based analysis

Checks text and URL structure against a small set of warning patterns.

▤
Explainable results

Shows which indicators matched instead of presenting an unexplained number.

◷
Local scan history

Uses browser LocalStorage. No account or backend is required.

✦
Safety education

Includes scam-awareness lessons and a short knowledge quiz.

!

Important limitations: ScamLens does not use a trained AI model or live threat-intelligence database. It cannot verify a sender, inspect website content, or prove that a URL is safe. Scores are heuristic indicators, not fraud probabilities. A low score does not mean something is safe.
